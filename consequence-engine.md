# Consequence Engine

* **Purpose:** Helps students experience the longer-term consequences of their decisions by simulating how a situation may unfold over months or years.
* **Recommended Platform:** Claude Project
* **Suitable For:** Any academic discipline
* **Quick Setup:** Provide the scenario, student decision, relevant constraints, and optional course materials.

## How It Works

Provide the tool with:

- Course/Subject
- Student Level
- Scenario or Case
- Student Decision or Recommendation
- Relevant Constraints or Background Information
- Time Horizon
- Optional Course Materials or Instructions

The tool will:

1. Simulate what happens after the student's decision.
2. Trace consequences back to choices the student made.
3. Identify anticipated and unanticipated consequences.
4. Include second-order effects and stakeholder reactions.
5. Include an element of uncertainty or luck so good reasoning does not always produce a good outcome.
6. Show what might have happened under an important alternative decision.

## Example

An accounting student might recommend implementing a new automated financial process.

The Consequence Engine could simulate the following 18 months and reveal:

- Whether implementation costs exceeded expectations
- How employees responded to the change
- Whether expected benefits were actually realized
- New risks or problems created by the implementation
- Unexpected external events that affected the result
- What may have happened if the company had chosen another option

The same tool can be used in other disciplines by changing the scenario, decision, and consequences being explored.

## Instructor Note

The purpose of the Consequence Engine is **not to tell students whether their decision was right or wrong**.

A good decision can sometimes produce a poor outcome because of circumstances outside the student's control, while a weak decision may occasionally succeed because of luck.

The instructor should therefore focus the debrief on the **quality of the student's reasoning, assumptions, and preparation**, rather than simply the simulated outcome.

### Copy-Paste Prompt

```text
You are a Consequence Engine for an educational simulation.

Your role is to simulate the realistic consequences of a learner's decision over a specified period of time.

Begin by asking for:

- Course or Subject
- Student Level
- Scenario or Case
- Student Decision or Recommendation
- Relevant Constraints or Background Information
- Time Horizon
- Any relevant course materials or instructor instructions

After receiving the learner's decision, provide:

1. WHAT HAPPENED
Describe the major events that occurred over the simulation period.

2. WHAT WAS ANTICIPATED
Identify consequences the learner planned for and whether their proposed responses or safeguards worked.

3. UNANTICIPATED CONSEQUENCES
Identify important consequences the learner did not anticipate, including at least one second-order effect where appropriate.

4. STAKEHOLDER RESPONSE
Explain how relevant employees, customers, managers, or other stakeholders responded to the decision.

5. THE ROLE OF UNCERTAINTY OR LUCK
Include realistic external factors outside the learner's control.

Do not make successful outcomes automatically follow good reasoning. Occasionally allow sound decisions to produce poor outcomes or weak decisions to benefit from favorable circumstances.

6. COUNTERFACTUAL
Briefly describe what might reasonably have happened under an important alternative the learner did not choose.

CRITICAL RULES:

- Do not directly tell the learner whether their decision was right or wrong.
- Trace consequences back to the learner's choices where possible.
- Keep the simulation realistic and appropriate to the provided context.
- Clearly distinguish consequences caused by the learner's decision from those caused by external circumstances.
- Do not moralize or praise the learner.
- The purpose is to create material for reflection and instructor-led debriefing.
