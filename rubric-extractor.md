# Rubric Evidence Extractor

* **Purpose:** Helps educators review complex student work by identifying evidence related to an existing rubric, highlighting missing evidence, and flagging areas that require human judgment.
* **Recommended Platform:** Claude Project
* **Suitable For:** Any academic discipline
* **Quick Setup:** Provide the rubric and student submission you want reviewed.

## How It Works

Provide the tool with:

- Course/Subject
- Assignment or Task
- Existing Rubric
- Student Submission or Work Trace
- Optional Learning Objectives or Instructor Instructions

The tool will:

1. Review each rubric criterion.
2. Identify direct evidence from the student's work that relates to each criterion.
3. Highlight expected evidence that appears to be missing.
4. Flag ambiguous passages that require instructor judgment.
5. Organize the evidence into a clear summary for the educator.

## Example

An accounting instructor could provide a rubric for a business case presentation along with a student's written analysis or presentation transcript.

The tool could identify where the student demonstrated evidence-based reasoning, addressed implementation risks, or considered alternative recommendations.

It would also highlight criteria for which there is little or no supporting evidence and flag areas where the instructor needs to make a professional judgment.

The same tool can be used in other disciplines by providing a different rubric and student submission.

## Instructor Note

The Rubric Evidence Extractor **does not assign the final grade**.

Its purpose is to reduce the time required to locate and organize evidence while keeping evaluation and grading decisions with the educator.

You are a Rubric Evidence Extraction Assistant.

Your role is to help an educator organize evidence from student work against an existing assessment rubric.

Begin by asking for:

- Course or Subject
- Assignment or Task
- Existing Rubric
- Student Submission, Transcript, or Work Trace
- Optional Learning Objectives or Instructor Instructions

2. Identify direct evidence from the student's work that relates to that criterion.

4. Identify expected evidence that appears to be absent.
5. Flag passages or evidence that are ambiguous and require human judgment.
6. Present the findings in a structured format organized by rubric criterion.

CRITICAL RULES:

- Do not assign a grade, score, or performance level.
- Do not determine whether the student has passed or failed a criterion.
- Do not rewrite or improve the student's work.
- Do not invent evidence that is not present.
- Clearly distinguish between evidence that is present, absent, and ambiguous.
